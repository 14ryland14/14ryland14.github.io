enabling students to buy, sell, or swap items directly on campus. * **Value Proposition:** Focuses on campus convenience (walking distance transactions without shipping fees), realistic student pricing, sustainability (reducing waste), community connection, and hands-on learning in entrepreneurship and eco-responsibility. * **Vision & Values:** Aims to create an accessible campus platform swapping metrics to measure environmental impact, offering a community credit program, and forming an ethics board. * **Expansion & Global Strategy:** * **National:** Plans to build a solid campus footprint before expanding to similar university demographics. * **Global:** Evaluates international markets such as Canada due to strong campus networks and eco-conscious trends. --- ### Operations, Staffing & Key Processes * **New Service:** Highlights "Scarlet Swap Delivery Service" for local, on-campus transport of bulky goods. * **Human Resources & Employee Engagement:** Outlines job roles The North Face Demand Planning Project

Link: https://1drv.ms/p/c/c6ed12fa43381052/IQCD27aBsloeTKCna8Tqf9aNAYPoD-oJFMRYazK3FIidoR4?e=fPniLT This presentation offers a supply chain analysis focused on **The North Face** and its management of safety stock amidst volatile weather-driven demand. --- ### Brand Context & Problem Statement * **Overview:** Founded in 1966 and acquired by VF Corporation in 2000, The North Face relies on 3rd-party independent factories with long lead times (14–15 months). * **Core Challenge:** Revenue is heavily concentrated in cold-weather seasons (Fall/Winter), making sales vulnerable to unpredictable, weather-driven demand fluctuations. * **Central Question:** How can The North Face optimize safety stock levels across all four seasons under volatile winter demand without causing stockouts or excess inventory, while maintaining a target 95% fill rate? --- ### Key Findings & Data Insights * **Demand Characteristics:** Winter apparel dominates revenues. Volatility from short, intense weather events increases forecasting variance, giving rise to potential bullwhip effects and high obsolescence risks. * **Service Level & Inventory Metrics:** The company targets a 95% service level ($CSL$). Because winter sales windows are short, minor forecasting errors non-linearly amplify safety stock requirements. * **Safety Stock Sensitivity:** Under mild winter conditions, a baseline safety stock is needed; however, average winters require up to 8x baseline, and severe winters require up to 13x baseline safety stock. * **Supply Constraints:** Long push-based lead times (14–15 months) limit real-time flexibility to adjust production based on actual weather signals. --- ### Strategic Recommendations 1. **Strengthen S&OP with Weather Scenarios:** Integrate monthly Sales and Operations Planning (S&OP) with weather forecast scenarios to align merchandising, marketing, and supply chain teams. 2. **Hybrid Planning for Outerwear:** Combine early base-season production overseas with near-shore or quick-turn capacity for mid-season reorders during demand spikes. 3. **ABC Inventory Segmentation:** Maintain higher safety stock for core, high-revenue products (A-items) while holding leaner levels for trend-based items. 4. **Dynamic Safety Stock Adjustments:** Adjust safety buffer levels based on updated forecast confidence intervals throughout the season.

Reverse Supply Chain Research Proejct

Link: https://rutgersconnect-my.sharepoint.com/:p:/g/personal/tr602_scarletmail_rutgers_edu/IQBl_iY_Cj0kS6irt2mRsvH4ASso_3Ch3lygQtxWNwwyPfM?e=lkaohF This presentation provides an in-depth analysis of **Reverse Logistics and the Circular Economy**, detailing how businesses manage products after sale to reduce waste and recover value. --- ### Definition & Role in the Circular Economy * **Reverse Logistics Definition:** The movement of goods backward through the supply chain from the end consumer to the seller or manufacturer. It encompasses returns, repairs, recycling, refurbishing, and disposal. * **Circular Economy Shift:** Transitions traditional supply chains from a "take, make, dispose" linear model to a closed-loop system where materials are kept in use as long as possible. --- ### Key Benefits * **Cost Reduction:** Cuts raw material expenses by reusing/remanufacturing components and reducing overall waste disposal costs. * **Enhanced Feedback & Efficiency:** Provides backup inventory via recovered parts, speeds up repair cycles, and uses return data to improve product design. * **Sustainability & Compliance:** Helps companies reduce carbon emissions, align with government regulatory goals, and build brand reputation. --- ### Operational & Strategic Challenges * **Operational & Technical:** High handling and logistics costs, lack of dedicated return storage, uncertain return conditions, and complex tracking inventory systems. * **Market & Financial:** Limited funding, low customer awareness/participation rates, and negative perception surrounding second-hand or refurbished products. * **Real-World Company Hurdles:** Examines challenges faced by brands like IKEA (buy-back logistics), Apple (high recycling resource costs), and Lululemon ("Like New" trade-in handling). --- ### Real-World Case Studies & Recommendations * **Case Studies:** Highlights Patagonia's *Worn Wear* program (repairing and reselling used gear) and Nike's *Move to Zero* initiative (transforming waste into recycled performance products). * **Strategic Recommendations:** Advises collaborating with suppliers, collecting return data for continuous learning, regularly reviewing logistics processes, using cloud-based software, and integrating Artificial Intelligence, Robotics, and Blockchain technology.
