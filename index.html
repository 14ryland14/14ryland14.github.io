Scarlet Swap Project

Link: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1U8MjnPCupo9rxC-MK5JbLGBPSXFlqpExKT_7kkSg1jI/edit?usp=sharing

This project that I worked on is Scarlet Swap, a proposed campus-centric peer-to-peer platform designed for Rutgers students to buy, sell, or trade items locally.

Core Business Concept & Value Proposition

  • Mission: Our goals is to establish a peer-to-peer eco-friendly economy at Rutgers by enabling students to buy, sell, or swap items directly on campus.
  • Value Proposition: We focued on campus convenience (walking distance transactions without shipping fees), realistic student pricing, sustainability (reducing waste), community connection, and hands-on learning in entrepreneurship and eco-responsibility.
  • Vision & Values: We aim to create an accessible campus platform while prioritizing eco-friendliness, community trust, inclusivity, and integrity.

Business Strategy & Goals

  • Financial Objectives: We target to reduce operational costs, $5,000 in seed funding for marketing and app development, and achieving positive cash flow within two semesters.
  • Strategic & CSR Objectives: We focus on active user growth, community participation, tracking swapping metrics to measure environmental impact, offering a community credit program, and forming an ethics board.
  • Expansion & Global Strategy:
    • National: We plans to build a solid campus footprint before expanding to similar university demographics.
    • Global: We evaluated international markets such as Canada due to strong campus networks and eco-conscious trends.

Operations, Staffing & Key Processes

  • New Service: We highlighted "Scarlet Swap Delivery Service" for local, on-campus transport of bulky goods.
  • Human Resources & Employee Engagement: We outlined job roles (e.g., Operations Manager), compensation models, recognition programs, flexible scheduling, and clear team communication practices.
  • Process & Effectiveness: We used SIPOC mapping (Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, Customers) and tracks efficiency indicators like cost per swap and user engagement rates.
  • Acquisition/Sell Decision: We recommended keeping ownership ("No Sell") to protect company values, employee interests, and long-term sustainability rather than selling to an outside buyer.

The North Face Demand Planning Project

Link: https://1drv.ms/p/c/c6ed12fa43381052/IQCD27aBsloeTKCna8Tqf9aNAYPoD-oJFMRYazK3FIidoR4?e=fPniLT

This project that I worked on offers a supply chain analysis focused on The North Face and its management of safety stock amidst volatile weather-driven demand.

Brand Context & Problem Statement

  • Overview: Founded in 1966 and acquired by VF Corporation in 2000, The North Face relies on 3rd-party independent factories with long lead times (14–15 months).
  • Core Challenge: Revenue is heavily concentrated in cold-weather seasons (Fall/Winter), making sales vulnerable to unpredictable, weather-driven demand fluctuations.
  • Central Question: How can The North Face optimize safety stock levels across all four seasons under volatile winter demand without causing stockouts or excess inventory, while maintaining a target 95% fill rate?

Key Findings & Data Insights From Our Research

  • Demand Characteristics: Winter apparel dominates revenues. Volatility from short, intense weather events increases forecasting variance, giving rise to potential bullwhip effects and high obsolescence risks.
  • Service Level & Inventory Metrics: The company targets a 95% service level ($CSL$). Because winter sales windows are short, minor forecasting errors non-linearly amplify safety stock requirements.
  • Safety Stock Sensitivity: Under mild winter conditions, a baseline safety stock is needed; however, average winters require up to 8x baseline, and severe winters require up to 13x baseline safety stock.
  • Supply Constraints: Long push-based lead times (14–15 months) limit real-time flexibility to adjust production based on actual weather signals.

Our Strategic Recommendations

  1. Strengthen S&OP with Weather Scenarios: Integrate monthly Sales and Operations Planning (S&OP) with weather forecast scenarios to align merchandising, marketing, and supply chain teams.
  2. Hybrid Planning for Outerwear: Combine early base-season production overseas with near-shore or quick-turn capacity for mid-season reorders during demand spikes.
  3. ABC Inventory Segmentation: Maintain higher safety stock for core, high-revenue products (A-items) while holding leaner levels for trend-based items.
  4. Dynamic Safety Stock Adjustments: Adjust safety buffer levels based on updated forecast confidence intervals throughout the season.

Reverse Supply Chain Research Project

Link: https://rutgersconnect-my.sharepoint.com/:p:/g/personal/tr602_scarletmail_rutgers_edu/IQBl_iY_Cj0kS6irt2mRsvH4ASso_3Ch3lygQtxWNwwyPfM?e=lkaohF

This project that I worked on provides an in-depth analysis of Reverse Logistics and the Circular Economy, detailing how businesses manage products after sale to reduce waste and recover value.

Definition & Role in the Circular Economy

  • Reverse Logistics Definition: The movement of goods backward through the supply chain from the end consumer to the seller or manufacturer. It encompasses returns, repairs, recycling, refurbishing, and disposal.
  • Circular Economy Shift: Transitions traditional supply chains from a "take, make, dispose" linear model to a closed-loop system where materials are kept in use as long as possible.

Key Benefits

  • Cost Reduction: Cut raw material expenses by reusing/remanufacturing components and reducing overall waste disposal costs.
  • Enhanced Feedback & Efficiency: Backup inventory via recovered parts, speeds up repair cycles, and uses return data to improve product design.
  • Sustainability & Compliance: Helps companies reduce carbon emissions, align with government regulatory goals, and build brand reputation.

Operational & Strategic Challenges

  • Operational & Technical: High handling and logistics costs, lack of dedicated return storage, uncertain return conditions, and complex tracking inventory systems.
  • Market & Financial: Limited funding, low customer awareness/participation rates, and negative perception surrounding second-hand or refurbished products.
  • Real-World Company Hurdles: Challenges faced by brands like IKEA (buy-back logistics), Apple (high recycling resource costs), and Lululemon ("Like New" trade-in handling).

Real-World Case Studies & Our Recommendations

  • Case Studies: Patagonia's Worn Wear program (repairing and reselling used gear) and Nike's Move to Zero initiative (transforming waste into recycled performance products).
  • Strategic Recommendations: Collaborating with suppliers, collecting return data for continuous learning, regularly reviewing logistics processes, using cloud-based software, and integrating Artificial Intelligence, Robotics, and Blockchain technology.
